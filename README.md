# Lipids do not support stand-alone, transportable risk stratification for diabetic retinopathy

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.23331501.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23331501)

Analysis code and **de-identified aggregate results** supporting the manuscript

> *Lipids do not support stand-alone, transportable risk stratification for diabetic
> retinopathy: a four-layer evaluation, with a cautionary finding on outcome
> misclassification* (submitted to *Frontiers in Endocrinology*).

This repository contains analysis scripts, figure/table-generation code and de-identified
**aggregate** outputs (`output_tables/`). It contains **no manuscript text (`.docx`) and no
individual-level data**. Individual-level data are held under the ethics approval described in
the manuscript (Bengbu First People's Hospital, approval BBYY2023072) and are not shared.
Rendered figures are regenerated by the scripts in `figures/`; four PNGs (`Figure1_study_design.png`, `Figure2_MR_forest.png`, `Figure3_calibration.png`, `Figure4_ROC.png`) are nevertheless tracked from the initial commit. They are aggregate plots carrying no individual-level data. To stop shipping regenerable binaries: `git rm --cached figures/*.png && printf 'figures/*.png\n' >> .gitignore`.

---

## Data roles (read this first)

The study's hypotheses were developed and tested in **data held by the authors' own
institution**, and the public resources are used only in supporting roles. This division is
deliberate and is stated on the first page of the manuscript, because several publishers now
require it for submissions built on public health databases.

| resource | role | where |
|---|---|---|
| Single-centre **institutional** screening cohort (Bengbu First People's Hospital EMR, 2019-2023; 2,482 records from 2,333 identifiers) | **hypothesis development and primary testing** | layers 2, and the coefficients exported to layer 3 |
| Public summary GWAS (GLGC; Neale lab UK Biobank ApoA-I/ApoB) and FinnGen R13 | causal premise only (a standing negative result) | layer 1 |
| NHANES 2005-2008 | **independent external validation** of the institutional coefficients, plus a locally fitted comparator benchmark | layers 3 and 4 |

NHANES is never used to search for associations. Layer 4 fits a reference model **locally in
NHANES** for one purpose only: to make the layer-3 transport test interpretable, by supplying the
one comparator that carries no transport assumption.

## The four-layer framework

| Layer | Question (hypothesis) | Scripts | Aggregate output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Causal premise (H1a) | Do lipids have an independent causal effect on DR? | `1_mr/`, `2_cis_mr/` | `MR_*.csv`, `MVMR_*.csv`, `cis_*.csv` |
| 2 — Discrimination (H1b) | Can a lipid score discriminate DR in the institutional cohort? | `3_prediction/` | `layer2_*.csv` |
| 3 — Transportability (H1c) | Do the institutional coefficients generalise to NHANES? | `4_external/` | `layer3_*.csv` |
| 4 — Benchmark (H4) | What *does* discriminate DR? | `3_prediction/`, `4_external/` | `layer4_*.csv` |

The design also adjudicates the two analytical conventions named in the manuscript (H2' and H3);
both are met by the same four layers rather than by a separate analysis.

## Key quantitative findings (aggregate)

All layer-2 to layer-4 numbers come from the **rebuilt outcome**: the DR grade was reconstructed
from the source ophthalmology text after the derived ordinal severity field was found to store
non-proliferative DR in the code reserved for ungradable photographs.

### Outcome reconstruction

The defective field is documented record by record in `layer2_outcome_crosstab_full.csv`. Its
single-page summary is this: of the **475** records carrying the value the pipeline read as an
ungradable photograph, **474** are true DR cases; and of the **275** records analysed as cases,
**none** carries a gradeable DR diagnosis — 50 are grade 0 and 225 cannot be graded at all. The
two classifications are therefore close to inverted.

Rebuilding from the source text gives 1,971 gradable records and **474** DR events (24.0%),
including 24 cases of proliferative DR. A record-level re-derivation by a structurally
independent implementation agrees completely (`layer2_independent_reextraction_summary.csv`).

> **Note on an earlier misreading.** `layer2_outcome_rebuild_crosstab.csv` is retained for
> provenance only. Its cells are labels, not counts, and reading them as counts produced the
> "a handful of cases / several hundred misclassified non-cases" account that appeared in an
> earlier draft. That account is wrong. See `output_tables/PROVENANCE.md` and do not quote the
> older file.

### Causal (layer 1)

Univariable TG OR 0.86 (p = 0.009) does not survive Bonferroni correction (0.05/6 = 0.0083),
attenuates to 0.93 (0.83-1.03, p = 0.18) after multivariable MR on a common set of 133
instruments, and is not reproduced by any of six cis drug-target analyses. MR-PRESSO outlier correction returns 0.878 (p = 0.0045), so the
multiplicity verdict depends on which analysis is treated as primary. Two settings give a
negative, nominally significant estimate; one gives the null; one gives the opposite sign. The
LPL cis instruments place the TG estimate on the **opposite** side of the null from the
genome-wide instruments (1.267 versus 0.86), and neither is robust, which is why the paper
describes **two opposite directions** produced by instrument choice rather than a reversal of a
real effect.

Under a more strictly defined control set (`MR_exmore_sensitivity.csv`) the TG estimate is
materially unchanged (0.879, p = 0.039).

### Discrimination (layer 2)

Optimism-corrected complete-case AUC **0.579** versus **0.582** under multiply imputed analysis
(m = 50, maxit = 50; three seeds): the two handlings agree. The bootstrap difference on the
common complete-case subset is +0.004 (95% CI -0.004 to 0.012) — note the opposite sign to the
-0.003 difference between the two reported AUCs, because they are different estimands.

Inverse-probability-of-selection weighting reproduces the full-cohort event rate (0.2407 versus
0.2405) and leaves discrimination essentially unchanged (0.5984 -> 0.5990). A three-row
decomposition gives 0.579 / 0.597 / 0.582. Missingness is outcome-associated in the clinically
expected direction (OR 0.70 for at least one lipid missing, p = 0.0033). Single-lipid
coefficients are not interpretable on their own: the maximum variance inflation factor is 14.75,
and the fitted signs are not biologically credible.

With the defective outcome the same pipeline reported complete cases 0.668 versus
multiply-imputed 0.608 (a gap of about 0.06) together with protective-looking missingness. That
discrepancy, reported in an earlier version of this work as a missing-data finding, was an
artefact of the outcome variable. **This is the study's first transferable finding.**

### Transportability (layer 3)

Institutional coefficients applied to the NHANES 2005-2008 diabetes subgroup give AUC 0.475 /
0.478 (LASSO version 0.554), MEC-weighted 0.457 / 0.446, with 95% CIs including 0.5. The MEC-
weighted calibration slopes are negative (-1.92 and -2.44), so the transferred linear predictor
reverses direction in the target population rather than merely drifting in scale. An MI-pooled
export gives the same picture (0.517 / 0.518; submitted frame).

### Benchmark (layer 4)

Full NHANES reference model AUC 0.786 (weighted 0.804; temporal validation 0.784 / 0.775),
against 0.540 for a lipid-only model fitted locally in NHANES (0.5377 with the extended
variable set, on 357 participants) and 0.4674 for the transported institutional score. The increment over the locally fitted lipid-only model is **0.246**, of
which **0.072** is attributable to cross-population transport; the two components are
exhaustive (0.246 + 0.072 = 0.318).

> **These are the primary-analysis values.** Participants whose photographs could not be
> graded are treated as having a missing outcome, so every layer-3 and layer-4 estimate rests on
> the participants with ascertainable retinal status (`NHANES_outcome_conventions.csv`, row
> "treated as missing"; temporal validation in `NHANES_temporal_validation.csv`). The earlier
> convention, which counted ungradable photographs as non-cases, gave 0.498 / 0.493 for layer 3
> and 0.733 / 0.515 / 0.486 for layer 4; those values are retained only in the archived tables
> `layer3_external_validation_AUC.csv` and `layer4_three_row_AUC.csv`, which the audit still checks
> as provenance.


> **One committed row is defective, and it has been corrected here.** The
> `local_lipid_only_std` row of `layer4_three_row_AUC.csv` reports n = 888 and AUC 0.4861 for
> the locally fitted lipid-only model using the extended variable set (+TG, +LDL-C). Both are
> artefacts of an alignment defect in the `oof()` helper of `phase4e_local_v2.R`: `model.matrix()`
> silently drops rows with missing predictors, so for this model — whose terms are missing in 473
> of the 888 reference records — the fold-wise prediction vector was shorter than the index it was
> assigned to, and R recycled it with a warning instead of failing. The quantity exists on **415**
> records and equals **0.4988** (95% CI 0.4368-0.5575). The other three rows are unaffected and
> still reproduce exactly. Corrected values are in `layer4_three_row_AUC_corrected.csv`; the
> guard that prevents recurrence is in `4_external/phase10_local_comparator_increment.R` and in
> the patched `phase4e_local_v2.R`. The manuscript sentence that quotes "AUC 0.486" for this
> model must be changed; the conclusion it supports is unchanged.

### Sensitivity analysis for repeat screening

The cohort is a series of screening records: 2,482 records were contributed by 2,333 patient
identifiers, 137 of which appear more than once. Retaining only the most recent record per
identifier (2,333 records; 1,868 gradable; 453 events, 24.3%) leaves every conclusion unchanged —
complete-case versus multiply-imputed discrimination +0.005 (95% CI -0.004 to 0.014),
missingness OR for any DR 0.70 (p = 0.0063), core / std transport AUC 0.490 / 0.485 with
MEC-weighted calibration slopes of -1.65 / -3.19, and the full model's increment over a locally
fitted lipid-only model still 0.218. Files carrying `_dedup` in their name belong to **this**
sensitivity analysis and must never be quoted as the primary analysis.

## The two transferable findings

Both are stated in the manuscript as sources of analytical dependence that are usually accepted
without being tested. They are the reason this repository ships a self-audit rather than just a
set of scripts.

**1. A misclassified outcome can manufacture an apparent missing-data effect.** Because the
outcome enters the missingness model, the imputation model and the discrimination estimate
simultaneously, a miscoded outcome distorts the comparison at every point and will look exactly
like a missing-data effect — including the diagnostics that are supposed to exclude selection
bias. The practical rule: **do not interpret a complete-case/multiple-imputation discrepancy as
selection bias, regression dilution or anything else until the outcome definition has been
verified against the source records.**

**2. An MR estimate is a property of the instrument set, not only of the exposure.** One exposure,
four settings, four estimates, and two of them opposite in sign. The practical rule: **report
direction consistency across instrument sets and require it, rather than treating a single
significant estimate as support for a drug target.**

**3. Corollary for both.** Every reported discrimination and calibration value should carry an
interval, and a calibration slope should say whether it is apparent or optimism-corrected. The
layer-2 complete-case calibration slope in this repository is exactly 1.000 because it is the
**apparent** value, fitted and evaluated on the same records; it is not evidence of good
calibration in new data.

## Reproducibility

- **MR / prediction / external validation:** R 4.5.2 (`TwoSampleMR`, `MendelianRandomization`,
  `mice`, `pmsampsize`, `rms`, `survey`, `pROC`, `glmnet`).
- **Figures, tables and the audit:** Python 3.13 (`numpy`, `pandas`, `statsmodels`, `matplotlib`,
  `python-docx`, `Pillow`).
- Run order follows the layer numbering. Scripts suffixed `_v2` implement the corrected-outcome
  pipeline and read `dev_set_v2.csv`; `rebuild_outcome.py` and `build_dev_v2.py` construct that
  outcome from the raw screening records. Scripts suffixed `_dedup` implement the repeat-screening
  sensitivity analysis and read `dev_set_v2_dedup.csv`.

### Running the scripts

The individual-level inputs are **not** distributed. Every script that needs them resolves the
analysis directory through `tools/paths.py`, so one environment variable repoints the whole
pipeline:

```bash
export LDF_SRC=/path/to/analysis/dir        # bash / Git Bash
```
```powershell
$env:LDF_SRC = 'X:\path\to\analysis'        # PowerShell
```

`python tools/paths.py` prints the active configuration and tells you which inputs are present.
A missing input raises a message naming the variable to set rather than a bare
`FileNotFoundError`. Individual scripts still contain their own `OUT <-` / `SRC =` assignment
from the original analysis runs; those are preserved for provenance.

### Before quoting any number

```bash
python tools/audit_repo_consistency.py
```

This re-derives every headline quantity from the committed aggregate tables (cohort accounting,
the inclusions-exclusion count, the AUC decomposition, every MR confidence interval against its
point estimate), gates the README against superseded wording, and exits non-zero on any failure.
Pass `--manuscript path/to/manuscript.docx` to cross-check the manuscript for the same numbers
and for known-superseded phrasings.

### Aggregate-table provenance

Most files in `output_tables/` are **not** written under those names by any script: the scripts
emit phase-named files into `phase*_out/` directories, and the `layer*` / `MR_*` names are a
documented selection of those outputs. `output_tables/PROVENANCE.md` (regenerated by
`tools/make_provenance.py` from `tools/artefact_map.json`) states for every file whether it is
written directly by a script, collected and renamed from a script output, hand-assembled, or held
locally only. It also flags the two files that must not be quoted.

### Environment note

On this machine the scripts must be run with the interpreter that carries the analysis packages
(`numpy` 2.4.4, `pandas` 3.0.3, `statsmodels` 0.14.6); a bare `python` on `PATH` is not

## Provenance notes

1. `phase2e_redo.R` (preserved as run on 2026-09-12) aborted inside its `pmsampsize` block, which
   passed both `csrsquared` and `cstatistic`; pmsampsize 1.1.3 accepts only one. It therefore never
   wrote four of its intended outputs. `phase2e_redo2.R` re-runs the identical analysis and writes
   them; `phase2e_tail2.R` completes the sample-size grid.
2. The derived severity field in the source records was populated by testing diagnosis keywords in
   a fixed order, with the proliferative keyword tested before the non-proliferative ones. Because
   the non-proliferative label contains the proliferative substring, almost every NPDR record
   matched the first test and was stored in the code the pipeline read as "ungradable".
   `rebuild_outcome.py` documents the corrected parsing rules; `layer2_outcome_crosstab_full.csv`
   reports the full cross-tabulation against the source text.
3. Two committed tables have no producing script and are assembled by hand; both are itemised in
   `output_tables/PROVENANCE.md`, and every value they contain is independently re-derived by
   `tools/audit_repo_consistency.py`.

## Pre-submission items (status)

Three items were listed here as open before submission. None of them is still outstanding.

1. **Outcome-side positive control** — done. Genetically predicted type 2 diabetes and the
   better-powered glycaemic panel (HbA1c, fasting glucose, fasting insulin) were run against the
   same DR endpoint, to show that the layer-1 instrument sets and the endpoint dataset can
   register a real effect. The 345 harmonised HbA1c instruments return OR 2.13 for type 2
   diabetes and 1.00 for DR; the fasting-glucose set returns 6.25 for type 2 diabetes and is null
   for DR. Producer: `1_mr/phase11_positive_control.py` (writes `MR_positive_control.csv`);
   reported in the manuscript's outcome-side positive-control section and in Supplementary
   Table S16.

2. **Replication of the layer-1 null in a second, independent DR outcome dataset** — done. The
   six-lipid layer-1 analyses were repeated against an independent UK Biobank diabetic-retinopathy
   GWAS (308 cases / 456,040 controls). Producer: `4_external/phase15_external_outcome_mr.R`;
   output `output_tables/layer1_external_ukb_dr_replication.csv`; reported in the manuscript.

3. **Blinded human re-abstraction of the source report text** — designed, but it could not be
   carried out. The sample, strata, sizes and design weights are specified and committed
   (`layer2_second_grader_sampling_summary.json`, `layer2_readset_subsets_summary.json`; producer
   `3_prediction/phase9b_second_grader_sampling.py`), but the material it requires — an earlier
   independent reading, or a record matching the platform's automated first-pass output to the
   final signed report — is no longer retrievable from the screening platform or the hospital
   information department. The source records therefore remain graded exactly as originally
   signed, and the manuscript reports the absence of a human re-abstraction as the principal
   limitation of the outcome definition. The layer-2 conclusion consequently remains conditional
   on a re-abstraction that these data cannot support.
